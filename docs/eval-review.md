## 2005 · Rules 1-9 · rule 1.2 · hard

**Q:** In a two-umpire game there is a close play at 3rd base. The base umpire calls the runner out, but the plate umpire saw the runner safe. Can the plate umpire overrule?

**Expected:** Yes, per the Manual. Rule 1.2(c) says umpires may consult with but not overrule each other, but the Manual (p5, p65) gives the plate umpire final say on 3rd and home and the base umpire final say on 1st and 2nd, and the only time an umpire may overrule another is when that umpire makes a call on a base outside their jurisdiction. In a three-umpire game each base umpire has final say on their own base (plate keeps 3rd and home); in a four-umpire game each umpire has final say on their own base.

## 2007 · Rules 1-9 · rule 1.1 · easy

**Q:** What acts count as "bringing the sport into disrepute" under the rules?

**Expected:** Rule 1.1 defines it as: any act intended to contravene the rules; any act designed to exploit gaps in the rules; any act of general anti-social behaviour; any act that may reasonably be foreseen to cause injury; and any act in contravention of the Four F's of Tee-Ball (Fun, Fair Play, Fundamental Skills, Family Involvement).

## 2018 · Rules 1-9 · rule 2.3 · medium

**Q:** A fielder is hit in the head by a thrown ball, gets up and says they are fine. Can they keep playing?

**Expected:** No. The umpire calls Time for the injury (6.1, 15c). Per the Manual (p9 under 2.3g, and p13 under 6.1), any player hit in the head by a ball, or who hits their head falling to the ground, must be removed from the game and sent for professional medical assessment, and must not re-enter until assessed as safe by a qualified medical professional.

## 2023 · Rules 1-9 · rule 3.2 · easy

**Q:** After a hit, when may the plate umpire put the tee back down on home plate?

**Expected:** Only after "Time" has been called and all runners have stopped running. Rule 3.2: the plate umpire MUST pick up the tee after each hit, fold it and hold it until then. The Manual (p10) stresses this as a safety rule ("FOLD IT & HOLD IT"), gripping the base and stem together so the tee can't swivel and hurt anyone.

## 2031 · Rules 1-9 · rule 5.1 · hard

**Q:** Runner on 3rd, batter hits to 1st. The 1st base coach steps out of the box and interferes with the throw to 1st. Who is out, the batter-runner or someone else?

**Expected:** The leading runner is out, i.e. the runner from 3rd, not the batter-runner (5.1b). The penalty for a coach outside the box interfering with play always falls on the leading runner.

## 2060 · Rules 1-9 · rule 8 · hard

**Q:** Does the umpire have to give a warning before removing someone for offensive conduct, like a coach's 5.1 box warning?

**Expected:** No. Under rule 8(a) (and 1.1), a first offender is liable to be removed at the umpire's discretion; a warning is optional, but a second offence by the same person must lead to removal. By contrast, some specific rules need at least one warning before removal or ejection: coaches leaving the coach's box (5.1a), incitement running (17.14) and the sitting-out rule (19.2).

## 2071 · Rules 1-9 · rule 9.3 · medium

**Q:** With the batting team's ninth batter at the plate, a fielder shouts "last batter, get this one!". The batter then hits a ground ball and is thrown out at 1st. What's the ruling?

**Expected:** The batter is credited with a home run: the indication was made by the fielding team (9.3c), so the throw-out does not stand. The batter must still touch all bases legally.

## 3045 · Rules 10-16 · rule 16.1(a) · easy

**Q:** A team lists 12 players. In the 1st innings, batters 1-9 bat and the umpire calls side away. Who leads off the 2nd innings?

**Expected:** Batter 10. Up to 12 players may be listed, only 9 bat per innings, and the next innings starts with the next listed batter (16.1a).

## 3052 · Rules 10-16 · rule 16.3 · easy

**Q:** The wrong batter steps into the box, and the scorers notice before 'Play Ball' is called. What happens?

## 3056 · Rules 10-16 · rule 16.3(c) · hard

**Q:** Batter 3 bats instead of batter 2 and singles. Batter 4 then comes up, 'Play Ball' is called, and batter 4 reaches 1st. Only now do the scorers report the error. Ruling?

**Expected:** Both hits stand. Batter 3 became legal when 'Play Ball' was called for the next batter (16.3b, c). The next batter is the one listed after the legalised batter, so batter 4 was correct, and batter 5 is next. Batter 2 misses that turn (Manual p33 scenario 6).

## 3057 · Rules 10-16 · rule 16.3(c) · hard

**Q:** Batter 3 bats instead of batter 2 and hits safely. Batter 2 then bats ('Play Ball' called) and also hits safely. Then the scorers report that batter 3 batted out of order. Who's legal and who should bat?

**Expected:** Batter 3 became legal when 'Play Ball' was called for the next batter (16.3c), so the proper next batter was batter 4. That makes batter 2 the improper batter. It was caught before 'Play Ball' for the following batter, so batter 2's play is nullified, runners go back, and batter 4 bats (16.3a, b; Manual p33 scenario 7).

## 3072 · Rules 10-16 · rule 16.9 · medium

**Q:** The batter carries the bat well past halfway to 1st, dropping it just before 1st, and beats the throw. Ruling?

**Expected:** Batter out and ball dead for not disposing of the bat by halfway to 1st. All runners return to their bases and the next batter is called up (16.9). The halfway point is the umpire's judgement and unquestionable. Manual p35 says don't be overly pedantic and invoke it when the carried bat has inhibited the fielder, but the Rule Book has no such condition and wins.

## 4004 · Rule 17 · rule 17.1 · hard

**Q:** The runner from 3rd steps over home plate without touching it and keeps running across the dead ball line to the bench. The plate umpire saw the miss. The catcher then holds the ball on home plate and appeals on that runner. What's the ruling?

**Expected:** Runner out for missing the base and the run does not count (17.7), provided an umpire saw the miss and the fielder correctly identified the runner on the first appeal, before 'Play Ball' on the next batter. The runner cannot come back to touch home because they have left the diamond (17.1); the Manual says 17.1 exists to stop runners re-entering after missing home plate.

## 4013 · Rule 17 · rule 17.3 · hard

**Q:** The runner from 1st and the batter-runner are both between 1st and 2nd when a thrown ball goes over the dead ball line. How are the runners placed?

**Expected:** Under the Manual's 17.3 exception, when two runners are on the same base path only the front runner goes on to the base they were going to (2nd). The back runner (the batter-runner) must return to the last base they crossed (1st).

## 4019 · Rule 17 · rule 17.4 · medium

**Q:** A runner left base before the hit, but the umpires were slow to call it and the fielders completed a double play. Do the outs count?

**Expected:** No. The infringement came before the hit, so the ball is dead from that point and no outs or plays count (17.4 note). All runners return and the offending runner is warned (or called out if it's their second offence that innings). The Manual says to call 'Time and Dead Ball' quickly, before an out can happen, to avoid this situation.

## 4040 · Rule 17 · rule 17.9 · easy

**Q:** Bases loaded, ground ball to the pitcher, who throws to the catcher. The catcher, holding the ball with a foot on home plate, receives it just before the runner from 3rd arrives. Out?

**Expected:** Yes, a force out. With the bases loaded every runner is forced (17.9), so tagging home before the runner arrives is enough (17.8a). The ball must beat the runner and the catcher must have control (1.2a).

## 4044 · Rule 17 · rule 17.11 · medium

**Q:** With 1 out, the runner on 1st stays on the base until a deep fly ball is caught, then runs to 2nd. Is that allowed, and how can the fielding side get the runner out?

**Expected:** Yes. After any catch the ball is live and runners may advance at their own risk (17.11). The batter is out, so the runner isn't forced, which means they must be tagged before reaching 2nd (17.8b); touching 2nd isn't enough. The Manual notes this is common on deep outfield catches.

## 4070 · Rule 17 · rule 17.16 · easy

**Q:** Close to 3rd base, a runner slides under the fielder's tag and reaches the base. Legal?

**Expected:** Yes. 17.16 note: a runner close to a base may try to slide under a tag. If the slide gets the runner to the base before the tag, they are safe.

## 5004 · Rules 18-22 + Manual umpiring · rule 18.1 / 17.16 · hard

**Q:** A fielder is fielding a slow ground ball right on the running line between 2nd and 3rd. The runner swings wide around the fielder. The fielding coach says the runner left the running line and should be out. Correct?

**Expected:** Not out for going around the fielder. A fielder fielding a batted ball has right of way and the runner must go around (Manual 18.1, p44); moving off the running line to avoid interfering with a fielder fielding a ball is not avoiding a tag (Manual 17.16, p43). Only if the fielder has the ball and is in a position to tag, and the runner deviates from the running line to avoid that tag, is the runner out (17.16).

## 5010 · Rules 18-22 + Manual umpiring · rule 19.2 · easy

**Q:** A player has sat out the 1st and 2nd fielding innings. What does the umpire do?

**Expected:** No listed player may sit out two consecutive fielding innings unless at the player's own request to the umpire (19.2). On noticing it, the umpire gives the coach one warning; on a second occasion the umpire removes the coach from the game.

## 5014 · Rules 18-22 + Manual umpiring · rule 19.3(e) · medium

**Q:** The 1st baseman sets up behind 1st base on the foul side of the line. Allowed?

**Expected:** No. From Play Ball until the swing no fielder except the catcher may be in foul territory (19.3e). The foul lines are fair territory (Manual 7.1h and Definitions, p71), so standing on or inside the line is fine. If the fielder gains an advantage from the violation, the umpire calls Dead Ball, returns runners and warns the fielder (19.3g).

## 5032 · Rules 18-22 + Manual umpiring · rule 20 · easy

**Q:** The 3rd baseman, without the ball and not fielding, stands in the base path between 2nd and 3rd. The runner has to slow down and go around but never touches the fielder. Can it be obstruction without contact?

**Expected:** Yes. Obstruction is a fielder not holding the ball and not in the act of fielding who impedes a runner's progress (20); 'impedes' does not need contact (Manual 20, p48). The umpire nullifies it, and the runner advances at least one base beyond where they were going.

## 5036 · Rules 18-22 + Manual umpiring · rule 20 · hard

**Q:** A runner from 1st collides with a fielder (no ball) standing on 2nd, is slow to get up and stops at 2nd while the ball comes in. Another runner was on 3rd. After Time, the umpires agree the runner from 1st would have scored without the collision. What is awarded?

**Expected:** Obstruction (20): the runner from 1st is sent home, and all runners in front of that runner also advance home (Manual 20 scenario, p48). The umpires come together after Time to decide where the runner would have reached; the award is at least one base beyond where they were going.

## 5056 · Rules 18-22 + Manual umpiring · Positioning: responsibility · medium

**Q:** In a two-umpire game there's a close play at 2nd. The base umpire calls safe, but the plate umpire thinks it was out. What happens?

**Expected:** The base umpire's call stands: the base umpire has the final say on 1st and 2nd, the plate umpire on 3rd and home (Manual 1.2c p5; Positioning p65). Umpires may consult but not overrule each other (1.2c). Any disagreement should be resolved by coming together quietly and quickly, never challenged in front of players.
